# Language coverage matrix

Working inventory of broad segmental teaching objectives, including selected contextual sounds and notation variants. Further dialectal and narrow transcription objectives remain open.

Each language cell combines expected usage with the current number of examples and word recordings.
Reference means included in the working language profile. Contextual, regional, loanword and notation cases are marked.
Outside profile means outside the selected reference scope; it does not rule out every dialect, borrowing or phonetic realization.
Recorded examples and reference usage are separate. An example count of zero is a content gap.
The profiles guide content selection. They are not a completed survey of every dialect.
Recording accent and pronunciation pairing remain pending unless explicitly reviewed.

## Transcription and accent notes

- /iː/ — English FLEECE and German long /iː/ need language-specific recordings. Current isolated preview is German.
- /ç/ — German contextual realization associated with front-vowel and other environments; teaching it separately supports phonetic recognition.
- /x/ — German back fricative realizations vary with environment; the narrow realization in Bach can be uvular [χ].
- /ts/ — Affricate objective, potentially written /t͡s/ with a tie bar.
- /pf/ — Affricate objective, potentially written /p͡f/ with a tie bar.
- /i/ — English /i/ and /iː/ can be alternate broad conventions for the FLEECE vowel; separate targets do not imply a universal phonemic contrast.
- /ɔ/ — English cot–caught patterns vary by accent; do not assume every US speaker maintains the contrast.
- /u/ — English /u/ and /uː/ may be alternate broad conventions. French /u/ is a plain vowel target.
- /ɑ/ — French /ɑ/ contrasts with /a/ only in some varieties. The present /ɑ/ target uses American English examples.
- /ʁ/ — French and German rhotics vary by speaker and context; this row covers uvular realizations.
- /ʏ/ — German short rounded vowel, contrasted with /yː/.
- /uː/ — Length-bearing English GOOSE and German long /uː/ need language-specific lessons.
- /œ̃/ — French /œ̃/ is retained in some varieties and merged with /ɛ̃/ in others.
- /ɔʏ/ — German diphthong notation varies across transcription systems.
